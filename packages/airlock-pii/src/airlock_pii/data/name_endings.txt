# What Kazakh given names are often made with at the end: Жұмабай, Тұрсынбек, Әбілхан,
# Нұржан, Сейтқали, Айгүл. A surname in the Kazakh style is such a name, an ancestor's. These
# are no sign of a name: too many ordinary words end the same way. They say one thing only.
# A word for a street or for a person may carry an ending ("көшесінде", "клиента"), and no
# ending of a case or of a plural looks like one of these, so a capitalized word that ends
# so is not such a word with an ending: "Көшербай" is a surname, and no form of "көше".
[given-kk]
бай
бек
хан
жан
ғали
қали
гүл
бике

# The same endings in Latin letters, as data/latin.txt spells them: Zhumabay, Zhumabai,
# Tursynbek, Abilkhan, Nurzhan, Seitgali, Seitkali and Seitqali, Aigul. In Latin letters a
# given name that is also a word ("Arman", "Kanat") is no person by its capital, and a word
# that ends so next to it is its surname; where a sentence opens with such a word before a
# given name, it is the surname too. Three spellings are left out, because too many words
# that are no names end so: "-han" (Afghan, Jonathan), "-jan" (Trojan) and "-bike".
# "-gali" and "-kali" also end a Russian verb in the past, typed in Latin letters
# ("pomogali", "iskali"), so they do not count for the word that opens a sentence.
