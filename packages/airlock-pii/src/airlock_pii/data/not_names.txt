# Words that look like part of a name and are none. Unverified: written from general
# knowledge. See README.md in this folder.
#
# Three kinds of word are here:
# - Ordinary words with the ending of a surname or a patronymic: "магазин", "документов",
#   "основа", "виновна", "admin". A surname is told by its ending, and these share it.
# - Words that open a sentence, where they have a capital like a name: "Вчера", "Бүгін",
#   "Please". Next to a real name such a word must not be taken for its other half. A
#   capitalized word before a given name is taken for its surname, at the start of a sentence
#   too ("Сейтқали Айгүл жазды"), so what a sentence commonly opens with has to be here:
#   "Сегодня Мақсат пришёл" has no surname in it.
# - In a section of their own, [surname-too]: ordinary words with the ending of a surname
#   that people are called as well ("калина", "рябина", "родина"). See that section.
#
# One word per line, in lower case, in its base form. For a Russian noun the base form is
# enough: "машина" also covers "машиной" and "машин".

# Ordinary words with the ending of a surname that are surnames as well: a berry and a
# tree, and people called Калина and Рябина. Such a word is read as a town that ends
# like a surname is (places.txt, [like-a-surname]). Alone it is the word: "Ингредиенты:
# Калина, Рябина, Осина" names no one. Next to a given name, a patronymic or initials,
# after a word for a person and after the field of a form it is the surname ("Анна
# Калина", "Фамилия: Калина"), and so it is in a list that also holds a surname alone
# which is neither such a word nor a town ("Участники: Иванов, Калина, Петров"). The
# entry stands for the surname of a man and of a woman in every case: "калина" for
# "Калин" and "Калиной", "сатин" for "Сатина".
